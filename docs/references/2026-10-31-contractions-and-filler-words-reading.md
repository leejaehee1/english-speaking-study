### Part 1. Contractions · 축약형으로 물 흐르듯 말하기 (2:53–5:09)

**David:** Okay, the first thing, and this one is simple but very powerful, is contractions.

**Alice:** Contractions. Okay, let's explain this for our listeners. What is a contraction?

**David:** So a contraction is when you take two words and you put them together. You make them shorter. You use an apostrophe. That's the little symbol, the little comma up in the air.

**Alice:** Yes. Like "don't." That is "do not," but shorter. Don't. Do not. Don't.

**David:** Exactly. And "can't." That is "cannot." Can't. Cannot. Can't.

**Alice:** And "I'm." That is "I am." I'm. I am. I'm.

**David:** Perfect examples. Now, here's the thing. When learners are nervous or when they are being very careful, they often say the full words. "I am happy. I cannot do that. I do not understand." And that's correct. Nothing is wrong.

**Alice:** Correct. But if you always speak like that, always using the full words, you start to sound a little formal, a little stiff. Native speakers use contractions all the time.

**David:** All the time. So, let me give some examples for our listeners.

Instead of saying "I am tired," say "I'm tired."

I'm tired.

Good. Instead of "I do not know," say "I don't know."

I don't know.

Instead of "she is not coming," say "she isn't coming."

She isn't coming.

Instead of "we are going to be late," say "we're going to be late."

We're going to be late.

You see how much more natural that sounds? It's smoother. It flows better. When you use contractions, your speech starts to sound more connected, more fluid.

**Alice:** Fluid. I like that word. Like water. It just flows.

**David:** Exactly. And here is a quick exercise for our listeners. Try to say these sentences first with the full words, then with contractions. Ready?

**Alice:** Ready. "I am not sure what you are talking about."

**David:** Okay, listeners, try to say that with contractions. Ready? Here it is.

"I'm not sure what you're talking about."

I'm not sure what you're talking about.

Perfect. Hear the difference? "I am not sure what you are talking about." That's the textbook version. "I'm not sure what you're talking about." That's the natural version.

And this is something you can practice right now, today. No special books, no special lessons. Just when you speak English today, try to use contractions. Try to put those words together.

**Alice:** Great advice. Let's move to the next one.

### Part 2. Filler Words · 자연스러운 추임새와 연결어 (5:09–8:15)

**Alice:** Okay, David. Next topic. This one is interesting because a lot of learners think filler words are bad.

**David:** Ah, yes. Filler words. People sometimes think, "Oh, filler words are mistakes. I shouldn't use them." But that's not completely true, right?

**Alice:** It's not. So, first, what are filler words? These are small words or sounds that people use while they are thinking, while they are deciding what to say next, like "um" and "uh."

**David:** Yes. "Um," "uh"—those are the classic ones. And then there are also words like "well," "so," "you know," "I mean," "like," right?

**Alice:** Oh, yes, "you know." I hear that one all the time.

**David:** All the time. And "I mean," that's a very common one, too. People say "I mean" when they want to explain something more clearly.

**Alice:** Can you give an example?

**David:** Sure. Someone might say, "I really liked the movie. I mean, it wasn't perfect, but it was really good."

**Alice:** Oh, so "I mean" is like saying "let me be more clear" or "what I'm trying to say is."

**David:** Exactly right. And "you know" is used when you think the other person understands what you mean. Like, "It was a long day, you know, I was really exhausted."

**Alice:** "You know," like checking if the other person understands. "You know what I mean?"

**David:** Yes. And "like"—now this one is very American English. Very common, especially with younger speakers. Like, "It was like the best thing ever," or "I was like really surprised."

**Alice:** I know this one—"like" everywhere.

**David:** Everywhere. Now, the important thing is don't use too many filler words. If you say "like" every three words, it becomes distracting.

**Alice:** Right. Everything needs balance. A few filler words make you sound natural. Too many make it hard to understand you.

**David:** Exactly. A few is natural. Too many is too much. Balance. But here's the point for our listeners. If you never use any filler words, if every sentence is perfectly clean and perfect, that can actually sound strange, too.

**Alice:** It can sound rehearsed, like you memorized a speech.

**David:** Yes, real conversation has small pauses, small sounds. "Um, let me think about that." That's natural. So, don't be afraid of "um" and "uh" and "well." They are part of real spoken English.

**Alice:** And "well" is a great one. "Well, I think the problem is..." "Well, let me explain." "Well, that's a good question." I love "well." It's such a useful word. It gives you a small moment to think and it sounds very natural.

**David:** So, let's do a quick practice.

**Alice:** Listeners, I will say something like a robot. No filler words, very stiff. Then, David will say it like a real person. Ready?

**David:** Ready.

**Alice:** Robot version: "I do not know the answer. I need more time to think about it."

**David:** Natural version: "Um, well, I'm not really sure, you know. I mean, I need a little more time to think about it."

**Alice:** Hear the difference? Both mean the same thing, but one sounds like a machine, the other sounds like a real person.

**David:** And listeners, neither is wrong. But if you want to sound more natural in conversations, practice adding a few of these small words and sounds.

## 자막 교정 메모

- **3:24 `And I'm that is I am. I am. I am. I am.` → `And "I'm." That is "I am." I'm. I am. I'm.`**: 앞선 `Don't. Do not. Don't.` 및 `Can't. Cannot. Can't.`와 똑같이 축약형과 원형을 번갈아 발음하는 구간인데 자동 자막이 `I'm`을 모두 `I am`으로 풀어 적은 부분입니다. 낭독할 때는 **I'm – I am – I'm**으로 대비해 읽으면 자연스럽습니다.

## 핵심 비교 · Robot Version vs. Natural Version

| 구분 | 문장 비교 | 포인트 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **축약형 전 (Textbook)** | **I am not sure what you are talking about.** | 단어마다 끊어 읽게 되어 다소 딱딱함 (`stiff`) |
| **축약형 후 (Natural)** | **I'm not sure what you're talking about.** | `I'm`, `you're`로 이어져 물 흐르듯 부드러움 (`smooth & fluid`) |
| **로봇 버전 (No Fillers)** | **I do not know the answer. I need more time to think about it.** | 문법은 완벽하지만 대본을 외워 읽는 느낌 (`rehearsed`) |
| **원어민 버전 (With Fillers & Contractions)** | **Um, well, I'm not really sure, you know. I mean, I need a little more time to think about it.** | 생각할 시간을 벌면서 훨씬 인간적이고 자연스러움 |

## 핵심 표현·한국어 뜻

- **contraction** (`/kənˈtræk.ʃən/`) — 축약형 (두 단어를 아포스트로피 `'`로 줄인 형태: `don't`, `can't`, `I'm`, `you're` 등).
- **apostrophe** (`/əˈpɑː.strə.fi/`) — 아포스트로피(`'`), 공중에 떠 있는 작은 쉼표 모양 기호 (`the little comma up in the air`).
- **Now, here's the thing.** — 자, 여기서 중요한 점은 이겁니다.
- **It's smoother. It flows better.** — 훨씬 더 매끄럽고 흐름이 좋아져요.
- **more connected, more fluid** — 더 자연스럽게 연결되고 물 흐르듯 유창한 (`fluid`: 액체·물처럼 매끄럽게 흐르는).
- **filler words** — 추임새, 연결어 (다음 할 말을 생각할 때 공백을 메워 주는 짧은 말들).
- **I mean, …** — 내 말은 ~ / 다시 말해 더 명확히 설명하자면 ~ (`let me be more clear`).
- **you know** — 있잖아 / 알다시피 (상대방도 내 말을 공감·이해하고 있는지 확인하며 이어갈 때).
- **It becomes distracting.** — 듣는 사람의 집중을 방해하게(거슬리게) 돼요.
- **Everything needs balance.** — 모든 것에는 균형이 필요해요.
- **It can sound rehearsed, like you memorized a speech.** — 마치 연설문을 통째로 외워서 말하는 것처럼(사전에 연습한 것처럼) 들릴 수 있어요. (`rehearsed`: 리허설한, 미리 외운 티가 나는)
- **Well, let me explain. / Well, that's a good question.** — 음, 설명해 드릴게요. / 음, 좋은 질문이네요. (생각할 시간을 1~2초 벌어주는 가장 유용한 단어)
- **Neither is wrong.** — 둘 중 어느 쪽도 틀린 것은 아닙니다.
