## 대화 대본 (Script)

### 챕터 1 · Introduction to the Video (0:00–1:07)

**David**: **Hello, hello, hello! Welcome back, everyone. Hi! We are so happy you are here today. Before we start, let me ask you one question.**

**Alice**: **Ooh, I love questions. What is it, David?**

**David**: **Do you ever feel like you understand English when you study it, but when you hear real people speak, you feel lost?**

**Alice**: **Oh yes, that happens to so many people.**

**David**: **Exactly. And that is why we made this video. Today, we are going to have real conversations—simple, natural, everyday conversations.**

**Alice**: **We will talk about things you do every day: going to the supermarket, talking to a friend, ordering food, talking about your morning...**

**David**: **And we will speak slowly and very clearly so you can follow every word. This video is your English listening practice, your speaking practice, your daily English podcast.**

**Alice**: **So sit back, relax, and listen carefully. If you are cooking right now, perfect. Keep cooking! If you are driving, eyes on the road, please.**

**David**: **Yes, eyes on the road, but keep listening! And if you are just relaxing, amazing. This video is for you. Let's begin. Are you ready?**

**Alice**: **I'm ready. Let's go!**

### 챕터 2 · Morning Routines · 아침 루틴과 모닝 커피 (1:09–4:27)

**David**: **Okay, Alice, let's start with the morning. The very beginning of the day. I love mornings... Well, sometimes.**

**Alice**: **Sometimes? That is very honest. Okay!**

**David**: **So, Alice, what time do you usually wake up?**

**Alice**: **I usually wake up at 7:00. 7:00 in the morning.**

**David**: **7 o'clock. That's a good time. Not too early, not too late.**

**Alice**: **Yes, exactly. I don't like waking up too early. If I wake up at 5, I feel terrible. 5 in the morning is very early.**

**David**: **Very, very early. I need my sleep! Okay, so you wake up at 7:00. Then what do you do?**

**Alice**: **First, I turn off my alarm. Then, I lie in bed for a few minutes.**

**David**: **Ah, yes. The famous "five more minutes."**

**Alice**: **Yes! Every morning, just five more minutes. But then 5 minutes becomes 10 minutes, and 10 minutes becomes 20 minutes, and suddenly I am late!**

**David**: **That happens to everyone! Okay, so after the five more minutes, what do you do?**

**Alice**: **I go to the bathroom. I wash my face, then I brush my teeth. Simple and important.**

**David**: **Let's say that again slowly. Listen carefully:**
- **I wash my face. I brush my teeth.**
**Very good. Now, do you take a shower in the morning?**

**Alice**: **Yes, I take a shower every morning. A quick shower, maybe 5 or 10 minutes.**

**David**: **A quick shower—that's a useful phrase. Quick means fast, short, right?**

**Alice**: **Right. I don't take a long shower in the morning. I don't have time.**

**David**: **I understand. Time is important in the morning. Okay, after your shower?**

**Alice**: **After my shower, I get dressed. I choose my clothes for the day.**

**David**: **And do you know what you want to wear, or do you stand in front of your wardrobe for 20 minutes?**

**Alice**: **Okay, sometimes I stand there for a long time. I look at all my clothes and I think, "I have nothing to wear!"**

**David**: **But you have a full wardrobe!**

**Alice**: **I know, I know. It doesn't make sense, but it happens. Everyone understands that feeling.**

**David**: **Okay. So, after getting dressed, what comes next?**

**Alice**: **Breakfast! The most important meal of the day. Do you always eat breakfast?**

**David**: **I try to eat breakfast every day. It is important. You need energy in the morning. What do you usually eat for breakfast?**

**Alice**: **Usually I eat toast with butter, or sometimes I eat eggs. And I always drink coffee. Coffee is very important.**

**David**: **Very, very important. I cannot start my day without coffee! I understand completely. Coffee is like fuel for the brain.**

**Alice**: **Exactly. Without coffee, I am not a person. I am just a zombie.**

**David**: **A zombie! Okay, let me repeat some of our key phrases. These are phrases you can use every morning. Great idea. Let's go slowly:**
- **I wake up at 7:00.**
- **I wash my face and brush my teeth.**
- **I take a quick shower.**
- **I get dressed and eat breakfast.**
- **I drink coffee in the morning.**

**Alice**: **Perfect. These are very simple phrases, but they are very useful. Practice saying them. Say them out loud!**

**David**: **Yes, say them right now. Pause the video if you need to practice. And now, let me ask you a question: What does your morning look like? Are you a morning person or do you need those five more minutes?**

### 챕터 3 · Supermarket Shopping · 마트 장보기와 계산 (4:30–8:07)

**David**: **Okay, now let's change the topic. Let's talk about something very common: going to the supermarket.**

**Alice**: **Oh, I love the supermarket. I go every week. How often do you go?**

**David**: **Usually twice a week. Two times a week.**

**Alice**: **Twice a week, good. Do you make a shopping list?**

**David**: **Yes, always. I always make a list before I go. If I don't make a list, I forget everything.**

**Alice**: **You go to the supermarket, you buy 10 things, but you forget the one thing you really needed!**

**David**: **Exactly! I go to buy milk, I come home with chips, chocolate, and juice, but no milk! That is a very relatable problem.**

**Alice**: **Very relatable. Okay, so let's imagine we are at the supermarket. Let's have a conversation like two friends shopping together.**

**David**: **Okay, I love this. Alice, do you have the list?**

**Alice**: **Yes, I have it here. Okay, we need bread, milk, eggs, some vegetables, and chicken.**

**David**: **Good. Let's start with the bread. Where is the bread section?**

**Alice**: **I think it's over there near the bakery. Ah, I can smell it—fresh bread!**

**David**: **Mmm, that smells so good. Should we get one loaf or two?**

**Alice**: **Let's get two. One for sandwiches and one for toast.**

**David**: **Good idea. Okay, now let's get the milk. What kind do you want? Regular milk or low-fat milk?**

**Alice**: **Let's get regular milk. I don't like low-fat milk. It tastes like water.**

**David**: **It does taste like water a little bit! Just a little bit. Okay, milk is in the cart. Now, eggs. How many eggs do we need?**

**Alice**: **Let's get a dozen.**

**David**: **A dozen. That means 12 eggs.**

**Alice**: **Yes, a dozen means 12. A dozen eggs = 12 eggs. Good, let's remember that: a dozen means 12.**

**David**: **Exactly. Okay, now vegetables. What vegetables are on the list?**

**Alice**: **Tomatoes, onions, and some lettuce for salad.**

**David**: **Great. Oh, look! The tomatoes look very fresh today.**

**Alice**: **Yes, they look beautiful. How many should we get?**

**David**: **Maybe six or seven. We eat a lot of tomatoes.**

**Alice**: **Perfect. And I need to check the price. Excuse me, how much are these tomatoes?**

**David**: **Oh, that's a very useful phrase: "How much are these?" Let's say it together:**
- **How much are these?**
- **How much is this?**
- **How much does it cost?**
**These three questions are very important when you shop.**

**Alice**: **Very important! Use them when you are at the market, the shop, or the restaurant. Okay, back to our shopping. We have the vegetables. Now let's get the chicken.**

**David**: **The chicken is in the meat section at the back of the store.**

**Alice**: **Okay, let's walk over there. And while we walk, let me ask: Do you prefer chicken breast or chicken thighs?**

**David**: **I like chicken breast for cooking. It's healthy.**

**Alice**: **Yes, chicken breast is very lean. That means it doesn't have much fat. Lean—that's a good word. Lean meat.**

**David**: **Lean meat. Not fatty, not oily. Good word! I'll remember that. Okay, we have everything on the list. Let's go to the checkout.**

**Alice**: **Checkout. That's where you pay.**

**David**: **Yes, the checkout. Or the cashier. The cashier is the person who takes your money. Let's go. Oh, wait... Should we get something extra? A treat?**

**Alice**: **A treat? You mean chocolate?**

**David**: **Maybe just a little chocolate. We deserve it!**

**Alice**: **Okay, okay. One chocolate bar and then we go.**

**David**: **Perfect. So, let's remember some key phrases from the supermarket conversation:**
- **I need to make a shopping list.**
- **How much does this cost?**
- **Let's go to the checkout.**
- **A dozen means 12.**
- **Can I have a bag, please?**

**Alice**: **Great job! These are phrases you can use right away. The next time you go shopping, try to think in English.**

### 챕터 4 · Talking About Work · 직업과 근무 형태 (8:11–11:25)

**David**: **Okay, let's talk about another very common topic. One of the most common questions in English is: "What do you do?"**

**Alice**: **Exactly. "What do you do?" This means, "What is your job?" It's a very simple question, but sometimes beginners don't know how to answer it in English. So let's practice. Alice, what do you do?**

**Alice**: **I am a teacher. I teach English to students.**

**David**: **A teacher. That is wonderful. Where do you work?**

**Alice**: **I work at a school in the city. Seeing students learn and improve makes me so happy.**

**David**: **That's beautiful. Okay, now you answer me. David, what do you do?**

**David**: **I work in an office. I am an accountant.**

**Alice**: **An accountant? So you work with numbers?**

**David**: **Yes, I work with numbers all day. I calculate money, I make reports, and I work with spreadsheets. But sometimes it can be a little boring.**

**Alice**: **Just a little! But I like my colleagues. My colleagues are nice people.**

**David**: **Colleagues—that's an important word. Colleagues are the people you work with. Or you can say co-workers. Same meaning! Okay, let's talk about work schedules. What time do you start work, Alice?**

**Alice**: **I start work at 8:30 in the morning and I finish at 3:30 in the afternoon.**

**David**: **That's a nice schedule! You finish early.**

**Alice**: **Yes, I like it. I have the afternoon free. What about you?**

**David**: **I start work at 9:00 and finish at 5:00. 9 to 5.**

**Alice**: **9 to 5. That's very common. Many people work 9 to 5. And sometimes people say "I work 9 to 5" to mean a regular, normal full-time job.**

**David**: **Now let me ask a fun question: David, do you prefer working from home or going to the office?**

**Alice**: **Ooh, good question! I prefer working from home. I can make my own coffee, and I can wear comfortable clothes.**

**David**: **Comfortable clothes? You mean pajamas?**

**Alice**: **Maybe! Just maybe.**

**David**: **I prefer going to the office. I like being around people. Working from home can be lonely. And at home, there are distractions.**

**Alice**: **Distractions—things that take your attention away from your work.**

**David**: **Like my cat! She walks on my keyboard, sits on my papers, and wants attention all the time.**

**Alice**: **Sounds like a very confident cat! Okay, let's review key phrases about work:**
- **What do you do for work?**
- **I work in an office.**
- **I start work at 9:00.**
- **I work with a great team.**
- **I prefer working from home.**
