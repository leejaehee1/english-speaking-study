### 1. Expressing Food Preferences · 좋아하는 음식과 싫어하는 음식 (9:25–11:54)

**Alice:** Okay. Now, let's learn how to talk about what you like and don't like when it comes to food, because this comes up all the time in conversation.

**David:** All the time. "What kind of food do you like? Are you a vegetarian? Do you have any allergies?" These questions are everywhere, and you need to be able to answer them naturally in real English, not textbook English.

**Alice:** So, let's go through the most important phrases for expressing food preferences. Phrase one, for expressing what you love: "I'm a huge fan of Italian food."

**David:** "Huge fan." That means you love it a lot enthusiastically. "I absolutely love sushi. I could eat it every day."

**Alice:** "I could eat it every day." Such a relatable phrase. "I could eat this every single day and never get tired of it."

**David:** And "I'm obsessed with"—a very modern, very casual way to express strong love for food. "I'm completely obsessed with this restaurant's tiramisu."

**Alice:** "Obsessed." It means you think about it constantly. You want it all the time. "I'm completely obsessed with street tacos. Whenever I travel to Mexico, that's all I eat."

**David:** That's all I eat. Complete dedication to one food. Obsession in its finest form.

**Alice:** And phrase two, for expressing what you dislike: "I'm not a big fan of"—a polite, gentle way to say you don't like something. "I'm not a big fan of olives, to be honest." Polite, not aggressive, just honest.

**David:** And "I'm not really into"—another gentle way. "I'm not really into very spicy food." "I'm not really into"—casual and soft, not offensive.

**Alice:** And if you really, really don't like something: "I really can't stand" or "I absolutely hate." But use these carefully. They are strong.

**David:** Strong. Use for dramatic effect or with friends who know your sense of humor. "I absolutely cannot stand coriander. It tastes like soap to me."

**Alice:** Coriander, or cilantro as American English speakers call it. And this is so true. Some people genuinely think it tastes like soap. It is actually a genetic thing.

**David:** A genetic thing? Really?

**Alice:** Yes. Some people have a gene that makes coriander taste soapy to them. It is science.

**David:** Wow. So when people say coriander tastes like soap, they are not being dramatic. Their taste buds literally experience it differently.

**Alice:** Literally differently. Isn't that fascinating?

**David:** Fascinating. And a great little food fact for small talk, huh?

**Alice:** Perfect small talk material. "Did you know coriander tastes like soap to some people because of their genes?" Instant conversation starter.

### 2. Dietary Needs & Restrictions · 식단과 알레르기 말하기 (11:54–13:11)

**David:** Okay. Phrase three, for dietary needs and restrictions. This is very important because more and more people have specific dietary needs, and you need to know how to express and discuss these in English.

**Alice:** "I'm vegetarian." You don't eat meat. "I'm vegan." You don't eat any animal products—no meat, no dairy, no eggs.

**David:** "I'm lactose intolerant." Your body cannot properly digest dairy products like milk and cheese.

**Alice:** "I have a gluten intolerance." Your body reacts badly to gluten found in wheat and many grains.

**David:** "I have a nut allergy." Very important. Allergies can be very serious. "I'm allergic to peanuts. I have a severe nut allergy."

**Alice:** "Severe." That means very serious, very strong. A severe allergy can be life-threatening.

**David:** Life-threatening. So, this is not small talk. This is very important information to communicate clearly and confidently.

**Alice:** Clearly and confidently. At a restaurant, don't be shy. Tell your server about allergies immediately.

**David:** Immediately. Your health is more important than being polite. Always.

**Alice:** Always. And a useful phrase at restaurants: "Does this dish contain nuts? Is there any dairy in this? Can you make this without gluten?"

**David:** "Can you make this without...?" Very useful. Very direct. And any good restaurant will be happy to help you.

**Alice:** Happy to help. Most restaurants understand dietary needs very well today. Just ask clearly.

**David:** Just ask. Always just ask.

### 3. Ordering at a Restaurant · 식당 입장부터 계산까지 (13:11–17:30)

**Alice:** Okay. Now, let's talk about ordering food in English at a real restaurant, because this is a situation that makes a lot of English learners very nervous.

**David:** Nervous? Even me sometimes. You don't want to order the wrong thing or not understand the server or mispronounce the dish name.

**Alice:** The dish name. Yes. Some dish names in English come from other languages—French, Italian, Japanese—and the pronunciation can be tricky.

**David:** Tricky. But don't worry, servers hear all kinds of pronunciations. They will understand you. And if they don't, you can always point. "This one, this dish right here."

**Alice:** Pointing is an internationally understood language.

**David:** Universal pointing. Okay, let's go through a restaurant conversation from arriving to paying. When you arrive, the host or hostess will greet you. They might say, "Good evening. Welcome. Do you have a reservation?"

**Alice:** "Reservation." That means, "Did you book a table in advance?" If yes: "Yes, I have a reservation under Smith for two people."

**David:** "Under Smith" means the reservation is in the name Smith. "I have a reservation under my name."

**Alice:** If no: "No, I don't have a reservation. Do you have a table for two?" "Do you have a table for two?" For two people? For three? For four?

**David:** And the host will say, "Of course, right this way, please." That means, "Follow me. I will take you to your table." "Right this way. Follow me." You walk behind them.

**Alice:** Then you sit and your server comes. They will often say, "Hi there. Welcome. My name is Sarah and I'll be taking care of you tonight. Can I start you off with some drinks?"

**David:** "Can I start you off with...?" That means, "What would you like first to begin with?" And you can say, "Yes, please. I'll have a sparkling water." Or, "Could we have a few minutes to look at the menu first?"

**Alice:** "Could we have a few minutes?" A very polite way to ask for more time. "Could we have a few more minutes, please?" And they will always say yes.

**David:** Always. Nobody will rush you. It is completely normal to ask for more time. Then when you are ready, the server comes back and they say, "Are you ready to order?"

**Alice:** "Are you ready to order?" And this is where many learners freeze. But don't worry, it is simple. You can say, "Yes, I think so," and then start with your order. Or if you are not ready: "Could we have just one more minute, please?" Again, polite, completely fine.

**David:** And when ordering, you can use several different structures. Listen carefully. "I'll have the pasta, please." "I'll have" is the most common way to order. Very natural, very clear. "I'll have the grilled salmon with vegetables, please."

**Alice:** Or, "Can I get the burger, medium, please?" "Can I get" is also very common, especially in American English. "Can I get the chicken salad, please? And can I have the dressing on the side?"

**David:** "On the side." That means put the dressing in a separate little cup, not on the salad, so you can add it yourself. "On the side." A very useful phrase. "Can I have the sauce on the side, please?"

**Alice:** And the server might ask you questions about how you want your food cooked, especially for meat. "How would you like your steak cooked?" And the options: rare, medium rare, medium, medium well, well done.

**David:** "Rare" means the inside is still very red and soft, barely cooked. "Well done" means cooked all the way through—no pink, no red. And "medium" is somewhere in the middle, a little pink in the center.

**Alice:** "Medium rare, please." That is a very popular choice for steak. A little pink, juicy, and tender.

**David:** "Juicy." When food has a lot of natural liquid and flavor, "juicy" is always a compliment for meat. "The steak was incredibly juicy." Yes, compliment.

**Alice:** And at the end of the meal, the server will ask, "How was everything? Did you enjoy your meal?" And you can say, "It was absolutely delicious. Thank you." Or, "Everything was wonderful." Or, "It was really good. We really enjoyed it."

**David:** And then: "Could we have the bill, please?"—British English—or "Check, please."—American English. Very casual, very common in the US. "Bill" in British, "check" in American. Same thing: you want to pay.

**Alice:** And the server might say, "Of course. Will you be paying together or separately?" "Together or separately?" That means one bill for everyone or individual bills.

**David:** "We'll pay together, please." One bill for everyone. "Could we pay separately, please?" Individual bills. And then the beautiful moment: you pay, you say, "Thank you so much. Everything was wonderful," and you leave full, happy, and with excellent English skills.

**Alice:** Full and happy. The best combination.

## 핵심 표현·한국어 뜻

- **I'm a huge fan of ~ / I'm completely obsessed with ~** — 나는 ~의 열렬한 팬이야 / 나는 ~에 완전히 푹 빠져 있어.
- **I'm not a big fan of ~ / I'm not really into ~** — (예의 바르고 부드럽게) 나는 ~을 별로 안 좋아해 / 내 취향은 아니야. (10월 27일 수업에서 배운 핵심 Chunk!)
- **I really can't stand ~** — 나는 ~을 정말 못 참겠어(너무 싫어해).
- **coriander (영) / cilantro (미)** — 고수. (`taste like soap`: 비누 맛이 나다, `taste buds`: 미뢰·입맛)
- **lactose intolerant / gluten intolerance / severe nut allergy** — 유당불내증이 있는 / 글루텐 불내증 / 심각한 견과류 알레르기.
- **Does this dish contain nuts? / Can you make this without gluten?** — 이 요리에 견과류가 들어가나요? / 글루텐을 빼고 만들어 주실 수 있나요?
- **I have a reservation under [이름] for two people.** — [이름] 앞으로 2명 예약했습니다.
- **Right this way, please.** — 이쪽으로 오시죠 (안내해 드리겠습니다).
- **Can I start you off with some drinks?** — 우선 음료부터 주문 도와드릴까요?
- **Could we have a few minutes to look at the menu first?** — 먼저 메뉴를 볼 시간을 몇 분만 주시겠어요?
- **Can I have the dressing (또는 sauce) on the side?** — 드레싱(소스)은 뿌리지 말고 따로 담아 주시겠어요?
- **Could we have the bill (또는 check), please?** — 계산서 좀 주시겠어요?
- **Will you be paying together or separately?** — 한꺼번에 계산하시나요, 따로 계산하시나요?
