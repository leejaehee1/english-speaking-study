## 대화 대본 (Script)

### 챕터 5 · Eating at a Restaurant · 식당 입장부터 주문과 계산 (11:34–14:32)

**David**: **Okay, now let's go somewhere fun. Let's go to a restaurant!**

**Alice**: **Ooh, yes! I love eating out.**

**David**: **Eating out means going to a restaurant to eat, not eating at home.**

**Alice**: **Right. We eat out maybe once or twice a week.**

**David**: **That sounds nice. So, imagine we walk into a restaurant. The waiter comes to our table. What does the waiter say?**

**Alice**: **Usually the waiter says: "Good evening! Welcome. How many people are in your party?"**

**David**: **"How many people are in your party?" Party here doesn't mean a celebration. It means your group!**

**Alice**: **Yes, your party means the people with you. So we say, "Just two, please." Simple and polite.**

**David**: **Then the waiter says, "Right this way," and they take us to our table. They give us the menu and we start reading.**

**Alice**: **And sometimes the menu is very long, and you don't know what to choose. I always take a long time. There are so many options!**

**David**: **I always ask the waiter, "What do you recommend?"**

**Alice**: **Ooh, that's a great phrase: "What do you recommend?" It means, what is good here? What should I order? The waiter usually knows the best dishes.**

**David**: **And sometimes they say, "Our special today is..." The special is a dish that is extra good that day, or a dish they make only sometimes.**

**Alice**: **"Today's special is grilled salmon with vegetables." Mmm, that sounds delicious!**

**David**: **It does. Okay, so we are ready to order. The waiter comes back. What do we say?**

**Alice**: **We say, "Can we order, please?" or "We're ready to order."**

**David**: **"We are ready to order." The waiter takes out their notepad and says, "Of course. What would you like?"**

**Alice**: **And I say, "I would like the grilled chicken, please, with a side salad." "I would like" is a very polite and natural way to order.**

**David**: **Very polite. You can also say, "I'll have the chicken, please."**

**Alice**: **"I'll have the chicken." This is also very common. Both are great. Both are natural.**

**David**: **And for drinks, the waiter will ask, "What would you like to drink?" And you can say, "I'll have a glass of water, please," or "Can I have an orange juice?"**

**Alice**: **"Can I have..." is another very useful phrase:**
- **Can I have the menu, please?**
- **Can I have more water?**
- **Can I have the bill?**

**David**: **The bill at the end of the meal—the bill is the paper that shows how much you need to pay. In the US, some people call it the check. In the UK, they say the bill.**

**Alice**: **So you can say, "Can we have the bill, please?" or "Can we get the check?" Both are correct. Both are natural.**

**David**: **And one very important phrase: If you need the waiter's attention, you say, "Excuse me." You don't shout, you don't wave loudly. You just say, "Excuse me" politely.**

**Alice**: **"Excuse me, can I have more water, please?" Very polite, very natural. Let's review the restaurant phrases:**
- **A table for two, please.**
- **What do you recommend?**
- **I would like the chicken, please.**
- **Can I have the bill, please?**
- **Excuse me.**

### 챕터 6 · Weekend Activities · 주말 휴식, 가족 모임, 집안일 (14:34–17:08)

**David**: **Okay, let's talk about something we all love: the weekend!**

**Alice**: **Oh, yes. Friday afternoon, the feeling when work is over—that is a wonderful feeling.**

**David**: **The best feeling! Okay, Alice, what did you do last weekend?**

**Alice**: **Last weekend, I relaxed a lot. On Saturday, I stayed home in the morning. I read a book and drank tea.**

**David**: **That sounds so peaceful.**

**Alice**: **It was very quiet, very relaxing. Then in the afternoon, I went for a walk in the park.**

**David**: **A walk in the park. Nice! Was the weather good?**

**Alice**: **It was a little cloudy, but not cold. A nice day for walking.**

**David**: **I love walking when it is cloudy. Not too hot, not too bright.**

**Alice**: **Exactly. And on Sunday, I visited my family. I went to my parents' house for lunch.**

**David**: **Oh, nice! What did you eat?**

**Alice**: **My mother made a big meal: roast chicken, potatoes, vegetables, and for dessert, chocolate cake!**

**David**: **That sounds amazing. Your mom is a great cook.**

**Alice**: **She is the best cook I know. No restaurant can compare to my mother's cooking!**

**David**: **That's so sweet. So you had a nice, relaxing weekend.**

**Alice**: **Yes, a quiet weekend. But I also did some chores.**

**David**: **Chores? Chores are the small jobs you do at home: cleaning, washing, etc.**

**Alice**: **Yes. I did the laundry. I cleaned the kitchen. I vacuumed the living room.**

**David**: **Very practical phrases. Let's say them again:**
- **I did the laundry.**
- **I cleaned the kitchen.**
- **I vacuumed the living room.**

**Alice**: **Now, let me ask you, David: Do you prefer a busy weekend or a quiet weekend?**

**David**: **Hmm, I like a mix. One day busy, one day quiet. On Saturday, I want to go out, see friends, do activities. On Sunday, I want to stay home and rest.**

**Alice**: **That sounds like a perfect weekend balance: work hard, play hard, rest well!**

**David**: **When someone asks, "How was your weekend?", remember to use the simple past tense:**
- **It was great! I went to the cinema.**
- **I had dinner with my friends.**
- **I stayed home and relaxed.**

### 챕터 7 · Health and Well-being · 증상 표현과 병원 진료 (17:15–20:20)

**David**: **Okay, now let's talk about health. This is a very important topic, especially when you are in an English-speaking country.**

**Alice**: **Yes, knowing how to talk about how you feel is very important. Alice, how are you feeling today?**

**David**: **I'm feeling great today! Very energetic. But sometimes we don't feel well. Sometimes we feel sick.**

**Alice**: **Imagine you don't feel well. How would you say that?**
- **I don't feel well.**
- **I feel sick.**
- **I'm not feeling well today.**

**David**: **Now, what specific problems might you have? Let's talk about common health problems:**
- **I have a headache.** (My head hurts.)
- **I have a sore throat.** (When it hurts to swallow or speak.)
- **I have a stomach ache.** (Pain in your stomach or belly.)
- **I have a cold.** (You sneeze, your nose runs, you feel tired.)
- **I have a fever.** (Your temperature is high, your body feels hot.)
- **I am exhausted.** (Very, very tired after a long week.)

**Alice**: **Okay, so now imagine you go to the doctor. The doctor asks, "What seems to be the problem?"**

**David**: **"What seems to be the problem?" This is what doctors say! And you answer:**
- **I have a headache and a sore throat. I think I have a cold.**
- **I have been feeling sick for two days.**

**Alice**: **This tells the doctor how long you have been sick. Notice the grammar point: `for` vs `since`:**
- **for 2 days** (length of time: 기간)
- **since Monday / since yesterday** (specific point in time: 시작 시점)
- **I have had a headache for two days.**
- **I have felt sick since Monday.**

**David**: **Now, let's also talk about general well-being. When someone asks, "How are you?", try going beyond "I'm fine, thank you":**
- **I'm feeling great today!**
- **I'm a little tired, but okay.**
- **I'm feeling a bit stressed, but I'll be fine.**
**That's real. That's how native speakers talk!**
