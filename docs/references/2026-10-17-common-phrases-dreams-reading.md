## Common English Phrases – Everyday Conversation

### 1. How Are You Doing?

**A:** Okay, let's practice some common English phrases, things you hear every day.

**B:** Great idea. Real phrases, real life.

**A:** First one, how you doing?

**B:** How are you doing? This is a very common greeting.

**A:** In everyday English, people say this instead of "How are you?"

**B:** They sound similar, but "How are you doing?" is more casual, more friendly.

**A:** And the answer, what do we say?

**B:** You can say, "I'm doing well, thanks. How about you?"

**A:** Or just "Pretty good, thanks." Or "Not bad. You?"

**B:** These are all very natural, very common answers.

**A:** Let's practice. I'll ask, you answer.

**A:** Ready, listeners?

**A:** Hey, how you doing?

**B:** Pretty good, thanks. How about you?

**A:** Perfect. See, very simple.

### 2. What's Up?

**A:** Okay, next phrase. What's up?

**B:** What's up? This is very informal, very casual.

**A:** Friends say this to each other.

**B:** It means, "What is happening? What is new?"

**A:** And the answer most commonly is just "Not much."

**B:** "Not much. You?"

**A:** Or "Just chilling," which means just relaxing.

**B:** What's up? Not much, just chilling. Very natural.

### 3. Never Mind / I Figured It Out

**A:** Next phrase, never mind.

**B:** Never mind. This is very useful.

**A:** It means, "Don't worry about it. Forget what I said."

**B:** For example, "Can you help me? Oh, wait. Never mind. I figured it out."

**A:** "Figured it out" means I found the answer. I solved the problem.

**B:** Both phrases are very useful. Never mind. I figured it out.

**A:** Say them slowly. Never mind.

**B:** Never mind.

**A:** I figured it out.

**B:** I figured it out.

### 4. Could You Say That Again, Please?

**A:** Next one. Could you say that again, please?

**B:** Oh, this is so important for English learners.

**A:** Very important. When you don't understand something, ask.

**B:** Don't pretend you understand.

**A:** Don't just nod and say, "Yes, yes."

**B:** We have all done that.

**A:** Yes. You smile and nod, but inside you're thinking, "I have no idea what they said."

**B:** Exactly. And it gets you into trouble later.

**A:** So instead, just ask, "Could you say that again, please?"

**B:** Or, "Sorry, I didn't catch that."

**A:** "I didn't catch that." This means I didn't understand. I missed it.

**B:** Or, "Could you speak a little more slowly, please?"

**A:** This is perfectly fine to say. Most people are happy to speak more slowly.

**B:** Yes, most people want to help you understand.

**A:** So don't be shy. Ask.

**B:** Asking is not a weakness. Asking is smart.

**A:** Asking means you want to learn, and that is a strength.

**B:** Beautiful.

### 5. Dreams and Fear of Failure

**A:** Alice, let me ask you something personal.

**B:** Oh, personal. Okay, I'm ready.

**A:** What is one dream you have? One thing you really want to do in your life?

**B:** Hmm. One dream. Just one?

**A:** Let's start with one.

**B:** Okay. One dream. I want to write a book.

**A:** Oh, really?

**B:** Yes. I have wanted to write a book for a long time.

**A:** What kind of book?

**B:** A story. A novel about real people, real life, real emotions.

**A:** That sounds wonderful.

**B:** But I have not started yet.

**A:** Why not?

**B:** Because I don't know where to begin, and I am a little afraid.

**A:** Afraid of what?

**B:** Afraid that it won't be good enough, that people won't like it.

**A:** Ah, that fear. Do you know what that is called?

**B:** What?

**A:** That is called fear of failure.

**B:** Fear of failure.

**A:** Yes. And almost everyone has it when we try something new and important.

**B:** Do you have it?

**A:** Yes, of course. When I start a new project, I feel it.

**B:** Really? Even you?

**A:** Yes, even me. What if it's not good? What if people don't understand it?

**B:** So, what do you do?

**A:** I start anyway. I begin even when I am afraid.

**B:** Even when you are afraid.

**A:** Because the fear does not go away by waiting. It goes away by doing.

**B:** That is very powerful.

**A:** So, Alice, start your book.

**B:** Just like that?

**A:** Just like that. Write one sentence today.

**B:** One sentence.

**A:** One sentence. Just one. And then tomorrow, write two.

**B:** Slowly but surely.

**A:** Slowly but surely. And one day you will have a book.

**B:** I will think about that.

**A:** Good. And listeners, what is your dream? What is one thing you really want to do?

Think about it. Take a moment.

And then take one small step towards it today.

Just one small step.

**One step is enough to begin.**
