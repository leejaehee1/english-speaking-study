### 챕터 9 · Positive Self-Talk · 내면의 목소리 바꾸기와 자기 격려 (27:40–30:51)

**David**: **Inside. The habit of how you talk to yourself. Oh, selft talk.**

**Alice**: **Selft talk. The conversation you have with yourself in your mind. Yes. The voice in your head.**

**David**: **Everyone has a voice in their head. And that voice, it says things to you all day. Like what? Well, sometimes it says positive things.**

**Alice**: **You can do this. You are doing well. Keep going. That sounds nice. And sometimes it says negative things. You're not good enough. You always fail.**

**David**: **You're stupid. Oh, that's harsh.**

**Alice**: **It is harsh. But many people have this kind of voice inside their head.**

**David**: **That's sad. Do people really say those things to themselves?**

**Alice**: **Yes. And the interesting thing is they would never say those things to a friend.**

**David**: **That's true. If my friend made a mistake, I would say, "It's okay. You'll do better next time." Yes. But to yourself, you might say,**

**Alice**: **"I'm so stupid. I can't do anything right." That's such a double standard. We are kind to our friends, but not to ourselves.**

**David**: **Double standard. Yes. That means using one rule for others and a different rule for yourself. And the rule for ourselves is much harder.**

**Alice**: **Much harder. And the problem is your brain believes what you tell it. Really?**

**David**: **If you tell yourself every day, "I'm not good at English," your brain starts to believe it. Oh no.**

**Alice**: **And then you stop trying because you already believe you can't do it. That's a terrible cycle.**

**David**: **Terrible cycle. A bad pattern that repeats itself. Exactly. But what if you change the voice?**

**Alice**: **That's exactly the habit we're talking about. Change how you talk to yourself. How?**

**David**: **Practice saying kind, encouraging things to yourself. The same things you'd say to a good friend.**

**Alice**: **Like, I'm learning. I'm improving. I made a mistake, but that's okay.**

**David**: **Yes. I'm not perfect, but I'm getting better every day. That sounds much nicer.**

**Alice**: **And it actually works. Research shows that positive selft talk improves performance. Improves performance. You do better.**

**David**: **Yes. Athletes use this. Students use this. People in business use this. And English learners can use this too.**

**Alice**: **Absolutely. Instead of saying my English is terrible, say my English is improving.**

**David**: **Instead of I can't understand, say I'm learning to understand.**

**Alice**: **Yes. Small change in words, big change in how you feel and what you do.**

**David**: **I want to practice this. It sounds hard though.**

**Alice**: **It is hard at first because your old negative voice is a habit and changing a habit takes time.**

**David**: **But with practice, the positive voice becomes the habit.**

**Alice**: **Exactly. You practice it every day. You catch yourself when you say something negative.**

**David**: **Catch yourself. You notice when you do it.**

**Alice**: **Yes. You notice and you say, "Wait, that was unkind. Let me say it differently." And slowly the positive voice gets stronger.**

**David**: **The positive voice gets stronger. The negative voice gets quieter.**

**Alice**: **I love that. The positive voice gets stronger. The negative voice gets quieter. That's the habit of positive selft talk. It quietly changes everything.**

**David**: **Quietly. There's that word again.**

**Alice**: **[laughter]**

**David**: **The title, the habits that quietly change your future.**

**Alice**: **And this one, your inner voice, changes your future in a very quiet way.**

**David**: **Yes, no one can see it. No one can hear it, but it shapes everything you do.**

### 챕터 10 · The Habit of Learning · 간격 반복과 근육 기억 & 실수에 대한 태도 (30:51–35:01)

**David**: **Okay, let's talk about the habit of learning because this is very relevant for our listeners.**

**Alice**: **Yes, learning English is a habit and today is a great example.**

**David**: **By listening to us, you are practicing English right now. That is a learning habit. Every time you listen to a podcast in English, that's a learning habit.**

**Alice**: **Every time you watch a video in English, learning habit.**

**David**: **Every time you read something in English, learning habit. Small, consistent actions every day.**

**Alice**: **And you don't need big dramatic study sessions. Short and consistent is more effective.**

**David**: **More effective than long study sessions sometimes.**

**Alice**: **Yes, 15 minutes every day is better than 2 hours once a week. Why? Why is 15 minutes every day better? Because your brain needs repetition.**

**David**: **Repetition means seeing something again and again.**

**Alice**: **Repetition. Yes. When I see a new word many times, I remember it.**

**David**: **Exactly. If you study a word once, you probably forget it.**

**Alice**: **But if you see it every day for a week, you remember it. It goes into long-term memory.**

**David**: **So, daily practice is more powerful than long, rare sessions.**

**Alice**: **Much more powerful. This is called spaced repetition. Spaced repetition. Spaced spread out over time. Repetition.**

**David**: **Doing it again and again. So you spread your study over many days instead of one big session.**

**Alice**: **Yes. And this works for languages, math, music, any skill. Any skill, not just English.**

**David**: **Not just English. Any skill you want to develop, practice it a little every day.**

**Alice**: **What other things can people learn with this habit?**

**David**: **Cooking, playing an instrument, drawing, coding, any skill.**

**Alice**: **So 15 minutes of piano every day, better than 2 hours once a week.**

**David**: **Yes. Your fingers, your brain, your muscle memory, they all need daily practice.**

**Alice**: **Muscle memory. When your body remembers how to do something.**

**David**: **Yes. Like riding a bike. After you learn, your body remembers. You don't have to think about it. That's muscle memory.**

**Alice**: **And you build muscle memory through regular repetition.**

**David**: **So daily practice builds muscle memory faster than occasional practice.**

**Alice**: **Correct. And this applies to speaking English, too.**

**David**: **Of course. If you speak English every day, even for 10 minutes, your mouth, your tongue, your brain, they all get used to it.**

**Alice**: **They all get comfortable with English and then speaking feels more natural.**

**David**: **More natural, more automatic, more confident.**

**Alice**: **Confident, feeling sure of yourself, not nervous.**

**David**: **Yes, confidence in speaking comes from practice, not from being perfect.**

**Alice**: **Not from being perfect. That's so important.**

**David**: **Many people wait until their English is perfect before they speak.**

**Alice**: **But that day never comes if you don't practice speaking.**

**David**: **Exactly. You become confident by doing, not by waiting. By doing, not by waiting. I love that. So, start speaking. Make mistakes.**

**Alice**: **That's okay. Making mistakes is part of learning.**

**David**: **Making mistakes is how you learn. Every mistake teaches you something.**

**Alice**: **I remember when I first started speaking English. I made so many funny mistakes. Like what?**

**Alice**: **I never forget. I know I'm full and I'm pregnant very well now. [laughter] Very important words to know the difference.**

**Alice**: **Now it's funny. That's the right attitude. Mistakes are funny later. They're also lessons.**

## 🎯 Part 4 핵심 청크 (Chunks) 6선

1. **be your own best friend** — 나 자신을 비난하지 않고 든든한 친구처럼 따뜻하게 격려하다
2. **I haven't learned it yet** — '난 못해' 대신 '아직 안 배웠을 뿐이야'라는 성장 마인드셋
3. **spaced repetition** — 망각 곡선을 이겨내는 뇌과학적 간격 반복 학습법
4. **confident by doing** — 자신감은 생각만으로 생기지 않고 직접 실천해봐야 생긴다
5. **a beginner's mindset** — 나이에 상관없이 항상 열린 마음으로 새롭게 배우려는 자세
