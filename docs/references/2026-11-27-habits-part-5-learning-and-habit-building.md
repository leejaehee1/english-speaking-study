### 챕터 10 · The Habit of Learning · 간격 반복과 근육 기억 & 실수에 대한 태도 (30:51–35:01)

**David**: **Okay, let's talk about the habit of learning because this is very relevant for our listeners.**

**Alice**: **Yes, learning English is a habit and today is a great example.**

**David**: **By listening to us, you are practicing English right now. That is a learning habit. Every time you listen to a podcast in English, that's a learning habit.**

**Alice**: **Every time you watch a video in English, learning habit.**

**David**: **Every time you read something in English, learning habit. Small, consistent actions every day.**

**Alice**: **And you don't need big dramatic study sessions. Short and consistent is more effective.**

**David**: **More effective than long study sessions sometimes.**

**Alice**: **Yes, 15 minutes every day is better than 2 hours once a week. Why? Why is 15 minutes every day better? Because your brain needs repetition.**

**David**: **Repetition means seeing something again and again.**

**Alice**: **Repetition. Yes. When I see a new word many times, I remember it.**

**David**: **Exactly. If you study a word once, you probably forget it.**

**Alice**: **But if you see it every day for a week, you remember it. It goes into long-term memory.**

**David**: **So, daily practice is more powerful than long, rare sessions.**

**Alice**: **Much more powerful. This is called spaced repetition. Spaced repetition. Spaced spread out over time. Repetition.**

**David**: **Doing it again and again. So you spread your study over many days instead of one big session.**

**Alice**: **Yes. And this works for languages, math, music, any skill. Any skill, not just English.**

**David**: **Not just English. Any skill you want to develop, practice it a little every day.**

**Alice**: **What other things can people learn with this habit?**

**David**: **Cooking, playing an instrument, drawing, coding, any skill.**

**Alice**: **So 15 minutes of piano every day, better than 2 hours once a week.**

**David**: **Yes. Your fingers, your brain, your muscle memory, they all need daily practice.**

**Alice**: **Muscle memory. When your body remembers how to do something.**

**David**: **Yes. Like riding a bike. After you learn, your body remembers. You don't have to think about it. That's muscle memory.**

**Alice**: **And you build muscle memory through regular repetition.**

**David**: **So daily practice builds muscle memory faster than occasional practice.**

**Alice**: **Correct. And this applies to speaking English, too.**

**David**: **Of course. If you speak English every day, even for 10 minutes, your mouth, your tongue, your brain, they all get used to it.**

**Alice**: **They all get comfortable with English and then speaking feels more natural.**

**David**: **More natural, more automatic, more confident.**

**Alice**: **Confident, feeling sure of yourself, not nervous.**

**David**: **Yes, confidence in speaking comes from practice, not from being perfect.**

**Alice**: **Not from being perfect. That's so important.**

**David**: **Many people wait until their English is perfect before they speak.**

**Alice**: **But that day never comes if you don't practice speaking.**

**David**: **Exactly. You become confident by doing, not by waiting. By doing, not by waiting. I love that. So, start speaking. Make mistakes.**

**Alice**: **That's okay. Making mistakes is part of learning.**

**David**: **Making mistakes is how you learn. Every mistake teaches you something.**

**Alice**: **I remember when I first started speaking English. I made so many funny mistakes. Like what?**

**David**: **Oh, no. Okay. One time I wanted to say, "I'm full after eating." But I said, "I'm pregnant."**

**Alice**: **[laughter]**

**David**: **What?**

**Alice**: **Yes. In my language, the word can mean both. And I confused them.**

**David**: **[laughter]**

**Alice**: **What did people say?**

**David**: **They looked at me very strangely and I didn't understand why.**

**Alice**: **[laughter]**

**David**: **Oh, that's wonderful. That's a perfect example of you make mistakes, you learn, and you never forget that lesson.**

**Alice**: **I never forget. I know I'm full and I'm pregnant very well now. [laughter] Very important words to know the difference.**

**David**: **Very important. But see, I made the mistake. I learned. And now it's funny.**

**Alice**: **Now it's funny. That's the right attitude. Mistakes are funny later. They're also lessons.**

**David**: **Lessons and funny stories. [laughter] Exactly. So don't be afraid of mistakes.**

**Alice**: **Make them, learn, laugh, move on. Make mistakes, learn, laugh, move on.**

**David**: **That's the learning habit. And that habit, that daily learning, that willingness to make mistakes, that quietly changes your future.**

**Alice**: **Your English gets better, your skills get better, your confidence gets better.**

**David**: **All from small daily practice every single day. Okay, Alice, we've talked about so many habits. Morning habits,**

### 챕터 11 · How to Build New Habits · 아주 작게 시작하기와 습관 쌓기 (35:01–40:15)

**David**: **Okay, Alice, we've talked about so many habits: reading, exercise, sleep, money, self-t talk, learning. Yes, we've covered a lot today. But here's the big question. How do you actually build a new habit? How do you make it stick?**

**Alice**: **Stick. That means stay, not disappear after a few days.**

**David**: **Yes. Most people try a new habit, do it for 3 days, and then stop.**

**Alice**: **That is so relatable. New Year's resolutions, anyone? Oh, yes. January 1st, I will exercise every day. January 8th, nothing.**

**David**: **Every year. Why does that happen?**

**Alice**: **Because people try to change too much too fast. Too much too fast.**

**David**: **They say, "Starting Monday, I will wake up at 5:00 a.m., go to the gym for 1 hour, eat perfectly, read for 30 minutes, meditate." That's exhausting to hear.**

**Alice**: **Yes, it's too much. So, after a few days, they're tired and they give up.**

**David**: **So, what's the right way to build a new habit? Start tiny. Start incredibly small.**

**Alice**: **How small? so small that it feels almost embarrassingly easy. Embarrassingly easy.**

**David**: **Yes. For example, you want to start exercising. Don't start with 1 hour. Start with 5 minutes. 5 minutes? Really?**

**Alice**: **minutes every day for 1 week. That's it. That sounds too easy.**

**David**: **That's the point. It's so easy you have no excuse not to do it.**

**Alice**: **H, that's clever. And most of the time when you start your 5 minutes, you end up doing more because once you start it's easy to continue.**

**David**: **Exactly. Getting started is the hardest part. Once you start, the momentum carries you.**

**Alice**: **Momentum, the energy that keeps something moving. Yes. Like a ball rolling down a hill. Once it starts rolling, it keeps going.**

**David**: **I love that image. The momentum of a rolling ball.**

**Alice**: **So start tiny. Create momentum and then slowly add more time, more effort. Slowly, not all at once.**

**David**: **Never all at once. That's how habits fail. Okay. Start tiny. What else?**

**Alice**: **Attach the new habit to something you already do. Attach like connect.**

**David**: **Yes. Connect your new habit to an existing habit. Give me an example.**

**Alice**: **Okay. You already make coffee every morning, right? Yes. Every morning. So, attach a new habit to the coffee.**

**David**: **While the coffee is making, do 5 minutes of stretching.**

**Alice**: **Oh, so the coffee triggers the stretching. Exactly. Trigger. Something that starts another thing.**

**David**: **The coffee is the trigger for stretching.**

**Alice**: **Yes. This is called habit stacking. You stack a new habit on top of an old one. Habit stacking. I like that term.**

**David**: **It works because your brain already has the routine of making coffee. You just add something new to it.**

**Alice**: **So, it's easier for your brain to accept the new habit.**

**David**: **Much easier. The brain says, "Oh, this is part of the coffee routine." That's really smart. Another example, after you brush your teeth at night, read five pages of a book.**

**Alice**: **Brushing teeth is the trigger for reading.**

**David**: **Yes, stack reading onto the toothbrushing habit.**

**Alice**: **This is brilliant. What else can help habits stick?**

**David**: **Track your progress. Keep a simple record of your habit. Like a habit tracker.**

**Alice**: **Yes. Every day you do your habit, put a check mark or cross out the day on a calendar. Why does that help?**

**David**: **Because you can see your progress. And humans love progress. It feels good to see the chain of check marks. the chain and you don't want to break the chain.**

**Alice**: **Exactly. Once you have seven check marks in a row, you don't want to stop. You don't want to break the chain.**

**David**: **That's so true. Psychologically, yes. The chain itself becomes motivating. You want to keep it going. So, track your habits. Keep a chain.**

**Alice**: **Don't break the chain.**

**David**: **And if you do break it one day, don't worry. Everyone misses a day. The important rule is never miss two days in a row.**

**Alice**: **Exactly. Never miss twice. One miss is an accident. Two misses is the start of a new habit. The habit of not doing it.**

**David**: **Oh, that's powerful. Never miss twice.**

**Alice**: **One miss is okay. Two misses is dangerous.**

**David**: **Yes. Get back on track the very next day. No guilt. Just start again.**

**Alice**: **No guilt. I like that. You said no guilt. That means don't feel bad about yourself.**

**David**: **Yes. Don't waste energy on guilt. Just start again. One more day.**

**Alice**: **Okay. Let's review the steps for building a new habit.**

**David**: **Please. I love a good review. Step one, start tiny. Start embarrassingly small. So small you can't say no.**

**Alice**: **Step two, attach it to an existing habit. Habit stacking. Use a trigger.**

**David**: **Step three, track your progress. Keep a chain. Check marks, calendar, anything visual.**

**Alice**: **Step four, if you miss a day, don't miss twice. Get back on track immediately. No guilt. Just restart. Four steps. Very simple.**

**David**: **Simple enough for anyone to follow. Even beginners.**

**Alice**: **Even beginners. Yes. And listeners, you are already doing one thing right today. What's that?**

**David**: **You listened to this whole podcast in English. That is a learning habit.**

**Alice**: **Yes, you practiced English today. Give yourself credit for that.**

**David**: **Give yourself credit. Recognize what you did well. Be proud of it.**

**Alice**: **You did something good today. Learning habit. Check. [laughter] Check. Day one of the English learning chain.**

**David**: **Yes. Don't break the chain. Come back tomorrow for more English practice.**

## 🎯 Part 5 핵심 청크 (Chunks) 5선

1. **spaced repetition** — 망각 곡선을 이겨내는 뇌과학적 간격 반복 학습법
2. **confident by doing, not by waiting** — 완벽해질 때까지 기다리지 않고 실수를 거듭하며 얻는 진짜 자신감
3. **start embarrassingly small** — 핑계를 댈 수조차 없을 만큼 민망할 정도로 작게 시작하라 (하루 5분)
4. **habit stacking** — 이미 굳어진 기존 습관(커피, 양치질)에 새로운 습관을 얹는 기술
5. **never miss twice** — 한 번 거르는 것은 누구나 하는 실수지만, 이틀 연속 거르면 나쁜 습관이 된다
