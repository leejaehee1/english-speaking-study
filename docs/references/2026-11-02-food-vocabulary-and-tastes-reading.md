### 2. Building Food Vocabulary · 8 Essential Taste & Texture Words (4:06–9:25)

**Alice:** Okay. First things first, let's build your food vocabulary. Because to talk about food, you need the right words, the right adjectives, the right expressions. And not just boring words like "good" or "nice." We want rich, expressive, real vocabulary.

**David:** Rich and expressive like good food itself. Exactly. Okay. Let's start with taste words. How food tastes, because taste is everything.

**Alice:** Taste is everything. Word number one, delicious.

**David:** Delicious. The most common food compliment in English. "This is absolutely delicious." "The food here is delicious." Delicious. Say it slowly. Delicious. Three syllables. Delicious.

**Alice:** And you can make it even stronger. "This is absolutely delicious. Incredibly delicious. Unbelievably delicious."

**David:** Unbelievably delicious. That is a strong compliment. It means the food is so good you almost cannot believe how good it is.

**Alice:** And a very casual, very enthusiastic alternative: "This is amazing." Or even, "Oh my goodness, this is so good."

**David:** "Oh my goodness," that is such a natural expression of surprise and delight. "Oh my goodness, this is so good." Say it. "Oh my goodness, this is so good." Say it like you mean it, like you just tasted something incredible.

**Alice:** "Oh my goodness, this is so good." I almost convinced myself something delicious was in front of me.

**David:** Method acting. Okay, word number two. Bland.

**Alice:** Bland. The opposite of flavorful. When food has very little taste, very little seasoning. "This soup is a bit bland. I think it needs more salt." Or, "The chicken was okay, but a bit bland for my taste."

**David:** A bit bland. Not terrible, just not exciting, not interesting. Like eating cardboard.

**Alice:** Eating cardboard. The ultimate bland food experience. Word number three, rich.

**David:** Rich, when food has a deep, strong, intense flavor, usually from butter, cream, or other fatty ingredients. "This chocolate cake is very rich. I can only eat a small piece." Or, "The pasta sauce was incredibly rich. So much butter."

**Alice:** Rich food is delicious, but sometimes too rich, too heavy, too much, too much of a good thing. Ha. Word number four. Savory.

**David:** Savory. This is a taste category. Not sweet, not dessert. Salty, spicy, herby. That is savory. Like pizza, pasta, grilled chicken, soup—these are savory foods.

**Alice:** And the opposite of savory is sweet. Cakes, chocolate, fruit, ice cream—sweet. Savory versus sweet. Two great categories. And many people, they love one more than the other.

**David:** "Are you more of a savory person or a sweet person?" Great small talk question at a dinner table.

**Alice:** Oh, great question. I am definitely savory. Give me chips over chocolate any day.

**David:** Really? I'm sweet all the way. Give me dessert first.

**Alice:** We are different people, David. Word number five, spicy.

**David:** Spicy. When food has a burning hot sensation from chili peppers or spices. "This curry is really spicy. My mouth is on fire."

**Alice:** "On fire." That expression, "my mouth is on fire." It means the food is extremely spicy. Not literally on fire, but it feels like it.

**David:** Feels like fire. "This is so spicy. My mouth is on fire." Say it. "My mouth is on fire."

**Alice:** And the opposite of spicy is mild. "Can I have the mild version, please? I can't handle spicy food."

**David:** "I can't handle." That means I cannot manage. I cannot tolerate. It is too much for me. "I can't handle spicy food. My stomach disagrees with it."

**Alice:** Your stomach disagrees. I love that. Your stomach has opinions.

**David:** Stomachs always have opinions. Word number six, crispy.

**Alice:** Crispy. Oh, I love crispy food. That satisfying crunch when you bite into something.

**David:** The crunch. Crispy fried chicken, crispy pizza crust, crispy roasted vegetables. Just saying the word makes me hungry. Crispy.

**Alice:** Crispy. And the opposite, soggy. When food is too wet and soft. Not crispy anymore. Soggy chips. Soggy bread. Nobody likes soggy.

**David:** Nobody likes soggy. That is a universal truth. Word number seven, tender.

**Alice:** Tender. When meat is soft and easy to cut or chew, perfectly cooked. "This steak was so tender, it melted in my mouth."

**David:** "Melted in my mouth." That is a beautiful food expression. "Melted in my mouth." It means the food was so soft and flavorful that it felt like it dissolved on your tongue. Like magic.

**Alice:** Food magic. "This lamb is so tender. It just melts in your mouth." Say it. "Melted in my mouth."

**David:** "Melted in my mouth." Delicious, just to say. And word number eight. Refreshing.

**Alice:** Refreshing. When food or drink makes you feel cool, clean, and energized. Usually something cold or light. "This lemonade is so refreshing on a hot day." "A fresh salad is really refreshing in summer."

**David:** Refreshing. Refreshing. Three syllables. It suggests renewal. Like your body is saying, "Thank you. I needed that."

**Alice:** "Thank you. I needed that." That is how refreshing food feels.

**David:** Exactly.
