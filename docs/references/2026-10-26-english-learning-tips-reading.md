**David:** Okay, Alice, let's talk about something very important for our listeners.

English learning tips. Yes, this is the topic everyone wants to know about. How do you really improve your English? What works?

I've thought about this a lot and I believe the most important thing is consistency.

Consistency. C O N S I S T E N C Y. Let's explain this word. Consistency means doing something regularly again and again. Not sometimes, but every day or almost every day.

Many people study English very hard for one week, then they stop, then they start again. This is not consistent and it's not very effective. It's better to study a little every day than to study a lot and then stop. 15 minutes every day is better than 3 hours once a week.

Exactly. Your brain needs regular input. Regular exposure to the language.

Exposure. E X P O S U R E. This means being in contact with the language, hearing it, reading it, using it. The more you expose yourself to English, the more you learn, even when you don't realize it.

That's an important point. You can learn English even when you are not studying.

Yes, you can learn by watching a TV show in English, by listening to music, by reading a menu in a restaurant, even by listening to us. Right now, you are learning. You are hearing real English conversation.

Your brain is working. Even if it feels relaxed, it is processing the language. That's the magic of listening.

Passive learning is still learning. Passive means you are not working very hard. You are just receiving, just listening. But even passive listening helps. Your ear gets used to the sounds of English, the rhythm, the stress.

Stress in English can mean worry, but in language it means which syllable you say stronger.

Good point. For example, comfortable. The stress is on the first syllable. Come for not comfortable. It's comfortable. These small things, stress, rhythm, sounds, you learn them by listening a lot.

Now, another tip. Don't be afraid of making mistakes.

This is huge. Many learners are afraid to speak because they are afraid to make mistakes.

But mistakes are how you learn. Every mistake teaches you something. When you make a mistake and someone corrects you, you remember it much better than if you read it in a book.

Exactly. Your brain remembers things that feel real, that feel important. So speak, talk to people. Maybe in person, maybe online. Just talk.

There are many apps and websites where you can find speaking partners. People who want to learn your language and they can help you with English. An exchange. You teach them, they teach you. Everyone wins.

Great idea. Another tip. Watch English movies and shows, but don't always use subtitles in your language. Try watching with English subtitles or no subtitles at all. It's difficult at first, but it gets easier and your listening improves so fast.

I agree. Subtitles in your own language, you read them, not listen. So, you're not really training your ears. But English subtitles, you hear the words and see them. Double learning.

Good advice. And one more tip, keep a vocabulary notebook. A vocabulary notebook is just a small notebook where you write new words. When you hear a new word, write it. Write the meaning. Write an example sentence. Then review it later. Go back and read your notebook. Test yourself. This helps you remember new words much better.

And one final tip, enjoy the process.

Yes. Don't think of English as homework. Think of it as an adventure. Every new word you learn is a tool. Every new phrase is a door that opens for you. When you speak English, you can communicate with millions of people around the world.

That's amazing. It's a superpower, really. English is one of the most widely spoken languages. It connects the world. So, enjoy learning it. Be curious, be patient, and keep going.

## 발음·자막 메모

- **Come for not comfortable. It's comfortable.**: `comfortable`의 강세가 첫 음절(**COMF-ter-bul** / `/ˈkʌm.fə.tə.bəl/`)에 있다는 점을 천천히 끊어 발음한 부분이 자동 자막에 `Come for`로 적힌 것입니다. 읽을 때는 첫 음절 **COM-**을 강조해 발음하고, 잘못된 강세(`com-FOR-ta-ble`)와 대비해 연습합니다.
