# What’s Really Worth Buying at Costco (And What to Skip)

Not everything in bulk is a good deal.

Shopping at Costco can feel like hitting the jackpot—huge carts, massive deals, and enough free samples to count as lunch. But not everything at Costco is a steal. Some items are totally worth it, while others might leave you regretting that bulk purchase. Here’s a breakdown of what’s actually worth buying and what you should leave on the shelf.

## Totally Worth It

These items are worth it, so stock up on these.

### 1. Rotisserie Chicken

This is hands down one of Costco’s best deals. For around $5, you get a big, juicy, ready-to-eat chicken that beats grocery store versions in both price and size. Plus, you can shred it for tacos, soups, or salads—endless possibilities.

### 2. Kirkland Signature Batteries

If you go through batteries like crazy (hello, TV remotes and kids' toys), Costco’s Kirkland brand is just as good as Duracell but way cheaper. Stock up and thank yourself later.

### 3. Gasoline

If your Costco has a gas station, you’re in luck. Their fuel prices are usually way lower than other stations, and if you’re already making a Costco run, it’s a no-brainer.

### 4. Organic Maple Syrup

Real maple syrup can get pricey, but Costco’s version is a steal. It’s high quality, lasts forever, and costs way less than what you’d pay at a regular grocery store.

### 5. Cheese

The cheese selection at Costco is chef’s kiss. You’ll find big blocks of high-quality cheese at a much better price than smaller grocery store packs. Great if you love charcuterie boards or just really love cheese.

### 6. Frozen Fruits and Veggies

Perfect for smoothies, soups, and quick meals, Costco’s frozen produce is fresh-tasting and comes in big bags that last forever. Plus, it saves you from tossing out sad, forgotten veggies from the fridge.

### 7. Olive Oil

High-quality extra virgin olive oil for a fraction of the price? Yes, please. Costco’s Kirkland EVOO is often ranked as one of the best out there, even compared to fancy brands.

### 8. Medications & Vitamins

The pharmacy section at Costco is seriously underrated. Over-the-counter meds, like ibuprofen and allergy pills, are way cheaper in bulk. Their Kirkland vitamins are also a great deal.

### 9. Wine & Liquor

Costco’s alcohol section is no joke. Their Kirkland-branded spirits and wines are shockingly good for the price, and some are even made by big-name brands (just with a different label).

### 10. Peanut Butter

If you go through peanut butter quickly, Costco’s two-pack of organic peanut butter is a solid deal. No weird ingredients, just good peanutty goodness.

## Maybe Skip These

You don't have to skip these completely but do some research before stocking up on them.

### 1. Fresh Produce (Sometimes)

Buying fresh produce in bulk sounds like a great idea—until you realize you can’t finish all those avocados before they turn to mush. Unless you have a big family or meal prep a lot, stick to smaller quantities elsewhere.

### 2. Giant Baked Goods

Yes, Costco’s muffins, cakes, and croissants are delicious. But unless you’re feeding a crowd, these massive packs can go stale before you finish them. Freeze them if you must, but otherwise, maybe pass.

### 3. Cereal

The big boxes seem like a good deal, but unless your household eats cereal every single day, it might go stale before you finish it. Plus, regular grocery stores often have sales that beat Costco’s price.

### 4. Spices

Spices lose their flavor over time, and Costco sells them in huge containers. Unless you’re running a restaurant, you probably won’t get through that giant jar of oregano before it tastes like dust.

### 5. Soda

Surprisingly, soda isn’t always cheaper at Costco. Grocery stores often run better deals, especially when they do BOGO (buy one, get one free) sales.

### 6. Canned Goods (Depends on the Brand)

Some canned goods at Costco are cheaper, but others are priced about the same as regular grocery stores. Check the price per unit before loading up your cart.

### 7. Pre-Made Meals

They look convenient, but some Costco pre-made meals aren’t great value. The per-pound price is often higher than making it yourself, and they can be hit or miss flavor-wise.

### 8. Clothing

Costco sells a lot of name-brand clothes at good prices, but sizing can be weird, and you can’t try anything on. If you don’t mind returning things, go for it—otherwise, maybe stick to stores where you can actually try stuff on.

### 9. Massive Jars of Condiments

Unless you’re running a sandwich shop, do you really need a gallon of mayo? Some condiments, like ketchup and mustard, are fine in bulk, but things like pesto or salad dressing can go bad before you use them up.

### 10. Diapers

If you have a baby, you’d think Costco is the best place for diapers. But sometimes Amazon’s Subscribe & Save deals or Target sales can be cheaper. Always compare prices before committing to a massive box.
