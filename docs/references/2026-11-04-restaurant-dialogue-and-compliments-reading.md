### 1. Complete Restaurant Dialogue · 식당 실전 롤플레이 (17:30–20:32)

**David:** Okay, everyone. Dialogue time. Let's put it all together in one complete restaurant conversation.

**Alice:** Listen carefully. Notice all the phrases we use and try to repeat after us.

**David:** And this time, really imagine yourself there at the restaurant with the menu in front of you. Picture it.

**Alice:** Picture it. Use your imagination and your English together.

**David:** Okay. I'll be the server. Alice, you and a friend are having dinner. Your friend is imaginary.

**Alice:** Ha. Ha. My imaginary friend. Okay, got it.

**David (Server):** Good evening. Welcome to Olive Garden. My name is David and I'll be looking after you tonight. Can I start you off with something to drink?

**Alice (Guest):** Hi. Yes, please. Could I have a sparkling water, and my friend will have a still water, please?

**David (Server):** Of course. Sparkling and still. Are you ready to order, or would you like a few minutes with the menu?

**Alice (Guest):** Could we have a few minutes, please? It all looks so good. I can't decide.

**David (Server):** Of course. Take your time. I'll be back in a few minutes.

**Alice (Guest):** Okay, David. I'm ready now.

**David (Server):** Perfect timing. What can I get for you?

**Alice (Guest):** I'll have the mushroom risotto, please. And could I get a side salad with the dressing on the side?

**David (Server):** Absolutely. And how would you like the risotto? Do you want extra parmesan on top?

**Alice (Guest):** Oh, yes, please. Extra parmesan always.

**David (Server):** Great choice. And for your friend?

**Alice (Guest):** She'll have the grilled salmon, please. Medium. And could you check if it contains any nuts? She has a nut allergy.

**David (Server):** Of course. I'll check with the kitchen right away. No nuts in the salmon. Can I get you anything else while you wait?

**Alice (Guest):** Actually, could we also get some bread for the table?

**David (Server):** Of course. I'll bring that right out.

**Alice:** "How was everything this evening?" Wait, now Alice is the server. Sorry, I got confused. You be the server again.

**David (Server):** Okay. How was everything this evening?

**Alice (Guest):** It was absolutely delicious. The risotto was incredible—so creamy and rich. I'll definitely be coming back.

**David (Server):** That's so lovely to hear. Can I get you anything else? Dessert? Coffee?

**Alice (Guest):** What do you recommend for dessert?

**David (Server):** Our chocolate lava cake is very popular. And the tiramisu is homemade. It's absolutely wonderful.

**Alice (Guest):** Ooh, the tiramisu, please. I can never say no to tiramisu.

**David (Server):** Excellent choice. I'll bring that right out.

**Alice (Guest):** Could we have the bill, please?

**David (Server):** Of course. Will you be paying together or separately?

**Alice (Guest):** Together, please. And thank you so much. The service was wonderful. The food was incredible.

**David (Server):** Thank you so much. It was our pleasure. Enjoy the rest of your evening.

**Alice (Guest):** Thank you. You too.

**David:** And that is a complete restaurant experience from arrival to goodbye in natural English.

**Alice:** Notice how many phrases we used: "Could we have a few minutes?" "Dressing on the side." "Could you check if it contains nuts?" "What do you recommend?"

**David:** "What do you recommend?" That is such a useful question. When you are not sure what to order, ask the server what they recommend. They know the menu best. They know what is fresh, what is popular, what is special today.

**Alice:** Always ask, "What do you recommend?" They love this question. They love sharing their favorites. And it often leads to the best meal of your life.

**David:** The best meal ordered by asking four simple words: "What do you recommend?"

### 2. 5 Ways to Compliment a Meal · 요리를 극찬하는 5가지 표현 (20:32–24:12)

**Alice:** Okay, now let's talk about complimenting food. Because if someone cooks for you—a friend, a colleague, a family member—you need to know how to tell them it is good. And not just "It's good." That is so flat, so basic.

**David:** So not enough. Not enough. Imagine someone cooks for hours. They prepare something special and you say, "It's good."

**Alice:** "It's good." That is almost an insult.

**David:** Almost. So let's learn how to really compliment a meal in a way that makes the cook feel proud and appreciated.

**Alice:** Phrase one: "This is absolutely incredible. What did you put in this?"

**David:** "What did you put in this?" That question is a double compliment. It means this is so good that I need to know the secret.

**Alice:** The secret. People love to be asked for their secret recipe. It implies your cooking is so good it must be magic.

**David:** Magic cooking, huh? And the cook will usually light up. They will want to tell you everything.

**Alice:** "Light up." Remember that expression from day four? "Light up" means become happy and excited, bright and energetic. The cook lights up because you showed real interest, real appreciation.

**David:** Phrase two: "I've never tasted anything like this before."

**Alice:** Oh, that is a strong compliment. "I've never tasted anything like this before." It means this is unique. This is special. This is unlike anything I have ever experienced.

**David:** And that is the highest compliment you can give a cook: your food is one of a kind.

**Alice:** "One of a kind." Unique. There is nothing else like it. "I've never tasted anything like this before." Say it slowly. "I've never tasted anything like this before."

**David:** Phrase three: "You should open a restaurant."

**Alice:** "You should open a restaurant." The ultimate compliment. It means your cooking is so good it deserves to be served professionally.

**David:** Professionally to paying customers. "This is restaurant quality." "This is restaurant quality food." Another great compliment: "You could charge for this."

**Alice:** Ha, "You could charge for this." That means your food is so good, people would pay for it. And people who love to cook, this compliment makes them so happy.

**David:** So happy. Try it next time someone cooks for you.

**Alice:** Phrase four: "I love what you've done with this."

**David:** "I love what you've done with this." It means this dish is familiar, but you made it special. You added something unique.

**Alice:** Like a classic dish with a creative twist. "I love what you've done with this lasagna. Is that a hint of nutmeg I taste?"

**David:** "A hint of"—a very useful food phrase. "A hint" means a very small amount, a subtle presence. A hint of garlic, a hint of lemon, a hint of cinnamon.

**Alice:** A hint, just a touch, just enough to notice. "I can taste a hint of something. What is that?" And the cook smiles mysteriously.

**David:** The mysterious cook: "That's my secret." And phrase five: "I'm going to need this recipe."

**Alice:** "I'm going to need this recipe." Not "Could I have the recipe?", not "Would you mind sharing the recipe?", but "I'm going to need this recipe." Like it is absolutely necessary. Like your life depends on it.

**David:** Like it is an emergency. "I am going to need this recipe or I cannot go on living."

**Alice:** That is dramatic, but cooks love drama when it comes to their food.

**David:** They do. "I'm going to need this recipe." Say it. "I'm going to need this recipe."

**Alice:** Say it with conviction like you mean it. Because you do.

## 요리 극찬 5대 표현 요약

1. **This is absolutely incredible. What did you put in this?** — 이거 정말 믿기 힘들 만큼 맛있네요. 여기에 뭘 넣으신 거예요?
2. **I've never tasted anything like this before.** — 살면서 이런 맛은 처음 먹어 봐요. (`one of a kind`: 세상에 하나뿐인 특별한 맛)
3. **You should open a restaurant!** (또는 **This is restaurant quality! / You could charge for this!**) — 식당 차리셔도 되겠어요! (완전 레스토랑 퀄리티예요 / 돈 받고 파셔도 되겠어요!)
4. **I love what you've done with this. Is that a hint of [재료] I taste?** — 이 요리를 이렇게 특별하게 살리신 게 정말 좋네요. 살짝 [재료] 향이 나는 것 맞나요? (`a hint of ~`: 은은하게 살짝 느껴지는 ~의 맛·향)
5. **I'm going to need this recipe!** — 이 레시피 저한테 꼭 알려 주셔야 해요!
