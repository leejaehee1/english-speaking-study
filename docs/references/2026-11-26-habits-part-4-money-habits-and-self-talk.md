## 대화 대본 (Script)

### 챕터 8 · Healthy Money Habits · 먼저 나에게 저축하기 & Need vs Want (23:12–27:40)

**Alice**: **Okay, David, let's talk about money now because habits affect money too, right?**

**David**: **Absolutely. And this is a topic that many people feel uncomfortable about.**

**Alice**: **But let's talk about it simply and honestly. Yes, money habits. What are they?**

**David**: **A money habit is anything you do regularly with your money.**

**Alice**: **Like spending it? [laughter] Well, yes, spending is a habit and saving is a habit.**

**David**: **And which is easier? Hm. Most people find spending easier than saving.**

**Alice**: **That's very true. Spending is so much fun.**

**David**: **It is. But here's the problem. If you spend everything you earn, what happens in the future? You have no money saved.**

**Alice**: **Exactly. And what if something unexpected happens? Like losing your job or getting sick.**

**David**: **Yes. If you have no savings, those moments become disasters.**

**Alice**: **Disasters. Yes, I can imagine. But if you have savings, they become problems you can solve.**

**David**: **Oh, that's a big difference. Disaster versus a problem you can solve.**

**Alice**: **One small habit, saving a little money every week, can change that. How much should you save?**

**David**: **Many financial experts recommend saving 10% of what you earn. 10%. Is that a lot?**

**Alice**: **Let's make it simple. If you earn $100, save 10. $10? That doesn't sound like much.**

**David**: **It's not. But if you save $10 every week, after one year you have over $500 from just $10 a week.**

**Alice**: **Yes. After 5 years, over $2,500. Just from $10 a week. Wow. Small habit, long time, big result.**

**David**: **Again, small habit, longtime, big result. This is a pattern.**

**Alice**: **It is a pattern. That's the whole message of today's episode.**

**David**: **I'm starting to really see it. All these small habits over time they create a completely different life.**

**Alice**: **Yes, a completely different life. That's the power of habits.**

**David**: **Okay. So, what's the key to the saving habit? How do you actually do it?**

**Alice**: **The best advice I know, pay yourself first. Pay yourself first. What does that mean?**

**David**: **It means when you receive money, put some aside for savings before you spend anything. Before you buy coffee, before anything?**

**Alice**: **Before anything. The first thing you do, save some money, then spend the rest.**

**David**: **So, you don't save what's left at the end. You save first and then spend.**

**Alice**: **Exactly. If you try to save what's left at the end, there's usually nothing left.**

**David**: **That is so true. I always think I'll save the rest and then there's no rest.**

**Alice**: **Everyone does that. That's why pay yourself first is such powerful advice.**

**David**: **Pay yourself first, save first, spend the rest.**

**Alice**: **And even better, make it automatic. Ask your bank to automatically move money to savings.**

**David**: **So you don't even have to decide. It just happens. Yes. Automatic. Like a habit.**

**Alice**: **You don't think about it. It just happens. That's brilliant. Make saving automatic.**

**David**: **And then you don't miss the money because you never saw it in the first place. Oh, that's a clever trick.**

**Alice**: **It's the simplest and most effective money habit. Save automatically, early, consistently. Save automatically, early, consistently.**

**David**: **I like these three words. Three words for three great habits within the saving habit.**

**Alice**: **You're very organized, David. [laughter] I try. Okay, but what about bad money habits? What should people stop doing?**

**David**: **Oh, good question. Bad money habits.**

**Alice**: **One very common bad habit, buying things you don't need because they are on sale.**

**David**: **Oh, no. That's me. [laughter] It's many people. You see something is 50% off and you think, "What a deal." Even if I didn't need it before.**

**Alice**: **Exactly. You didn't need it, but now it's on sale, so you buy it.**

**David**: **And you spent money on something unnecessary.**

**Alice**: **A sale is only a deal if you needed the item anyway.**

**David**: **If you needed it anyway. That's an important sentence.**

**Alice**: **A sale is only a deal if you needed the item anyway. Otherwise, you spent money you didn't need to spend.**

**David**: **Wow, that changes how I think about shopping.**

**Alice**: **Good. That's the goal. Think before you buy. Ask yourself, do I need this or do I just want it because it's cheap? Need versus want. Need versus want.**

**David**: **Two very important words. Need something necessary. Want something you desire but don't need.**

**Alice**: **Need versus want. A great question to ask yourself every time you want to buy something.**

**David**: **Every time. It saves you so much money over time. Okay. Now, let's talk about something a little different. Something more inside.**

### 챕터 9 · Positive Self-Talk · 내면의 목소리 바꾸기와 자기 격려 (27:40–30:51)

**David**: **Inside. The habit of how you talk to yourself. Oh, selft talk.**

**Alice**: **Selft talk. The conversation you have with yourself in your mind. Yes. The voice in your head.**

**David**: **Everyone has a voice in their head. And that voice, it says things to you all day. Like what? Well, sometimes it says positive things.**

**Alice**: **You can do this. You are doing well. Keep going. That sounds nice. And sometimes it says negative things. You're not good enough. You always fail.**

**David**: **You're stupid. Oh, that's harsh.**

**Alice**: **It is harsh. But many people have this kind of voice inside their head.**

**David**: **That's sad. Do people really say those things to themselves?**

**Alice**: **Yes. And the interesting thing is they would never say those things to a friend.**

**David**: **That's true. If my friend made a mistake, I would say, "It's okay. You'll do better next time." Yes. But to yourself, you might say,**

**Alice**: **"I'm so stupid. I can't do anything right." That's such a double standard. We are kind to our friends, but not to ourselves.**

**David**: **Double standard. Yes. That means using one rule for others and a different rule for yourself. And the rule for ourselves is much harder.**

**Alice**: **Much harder. And the problem is your brain believes what you tell it. Really?**

**David**: **If you tell yourself every day, "I'm not good at English," your brain starts to believe it. Oh no.**

**Alice**: **And then you stop trying because you already believe you can't do it. That's a terrible cycle.**

**David**: **Terrible cycle. A bad pattern that repeats itself. Exactly. But what if you change the voice?**

**Alice**: **That's exactly the habit we're talking about. Change how you talk to yourself. How?**

**David**: **Practice saying kind, encouraging things to yourself. The same things you'd say to a good friend.**

**Alice**: **Like, I'm learning. I'm improving. I made a mistake, but that's okay.**

**David**: **Yes. I'm not perfect, but I'm getting better every day. That sounds much nicer.**

**Alice**: **And it actually works. Research shows that positive selft talk improves performance. Improves performance. You do better.**

**David**: **Yes. Athletes use this. Students use this. People in business use this. And English learners can use this too.**

**Alice**: **Absolutely. Instead of saying my English is terrible, say my English is improving.**

**David**: **Instead of I can't understand, say I'm learning to understand.**

**Alice**: **Yes. Small change in words, big change in how you feel and what you do.**

**David**: **I want to practice this. It sounds hard though.**

**Alice**: **It is hard at first because your old negative voice is a habit and changing a habit takes time.**

**David**: **But with practice, the positive voice becomes the habit.**

**Alice**: **Exactly. You practice it every day. You catch yourself when you say something negative.**

**David**: **Catch yourself. You notice when you do it.**

**Alice**: **Yes. You notice and you say, "Wait, that was unkind. Let me say it differently." And slowly the positive voice gets stronger.**

**David**: **The positive voice gets stronger. The negative voice gets quieter.**

**Alice**: **I love that. The positive voice gets stronger. The negative voice gets quieter. That's the habit of positive selft talk. It quietly changes everything.**

**David**: **Quietly. There's that word again.**

**Alice**: **[laughter]**

**David**: **The title, the habits that quietly change your future.**

**Alice**: **And this one, your inner voice, changes your future in a very quiet way.**

**David**: **Yes, no one can see it. No one can hear it, but it shapes everything you do.**
